US natural gas: intraday seasonality and the EIA storage release

Two results from the research programme below, both in Henry Hub-linked natural gas (OANDA NATGAS/USD, 1-minute data, calendar year 2017).

1. Seasonality strategy with asymmetric exits

Entry
Long when both signals are positive, short when both are negative, flat otherwise. Signal A: mean hourly return over the trailing 24 hours. Signal B: mean return of the same clock hour over the trailing 30 sessions.
Exit
Stop at 1.0× ATR(14). Half the position taken off at 2.0× ATR. Stop on the remainder moved to breakeven, then trailed 1.5× ATR from the high-water mark. Exits resolved on 1-minute bars.

2. EIA weekly storage release: event study

The EIA Weekly Natural Gas Storage Report is released Thursdays at 10:30 ET. A scan of average absolute 1-minute returns by weekday and time identifies the release as the largest recurring intraday move in the series outside the Sunday reopen.

Measure (52 releases)Value
Average absolute move, first 5 minutes0.32%, approx. 7× a typical 5-minute move
Correlation, first 5 minutes vs. following 3 hours+0.15
Initial direction sustained over 3 hours56% (58% for above-median reactions)
Approx. 95% confidence interval42% to 70%

Caveats and next steps

  • The strategy clears break-even by 0.6 percentage points and is cost-sensitive: net return falls to −8.0% at 2 bp per fill.
  • The continuation result rests on one year of data; the confidence interval includes 50%. Out-of-sample testing on 2010 to 2024 is the next step.
  • Expressing the continuation view through long weekly options lost the full premium; option cost (approx. 3.0% of spot) exceeded the typical weekly move.
  • In development: a surprise model scoring weather (gas-weighted degree days), storage (actual vs. consensus) and supply (dry gas production, LNG feedgas) to forecast the storage print.

Research programme: intraday return seasonality, 14 iterations

Each iteration tests one change against real 2017 data in S&P 500, Bitcoin, WTI crude and natural gas. Weights were fitted only on prior data, transaction costs were included from the first test, and all results are reported, including negative ones.

1

Copper: 1 PM to 2 PM level forecast

Specification error
Hypothesis
2 PM price = 0.5 × mean of today's pre-1 PM prices + 0.5 × mean 2 PM close over 30 sessions. Buy calls if the forecast exceeds the 1 PM price.
Finding
Averaging price levels biases the forecast in trending markets: a 10% monthly rise places the 30-day average well below spot, producing a persistent sell signal.
Change
All subsequent models are specified in returns.
pred_2pm = p_1pm + 0.5*(morning_mean - p_1pm)
                 + 0.5*mean(close_2pm - close_1pm, last 30 sessions)
2

Two-factor model and walk-forward framework

Framework validated
Factors
A: cumulative return from the open to the start of the slot. B: mean return of the same 30-minute slot over the prior 40 sessions (after Heston, Korajczyk and Sadka, 2010, Journal of Finance).
Method
Factors standardised per slot; sign estimated from training-window correlation. Weight w selected by walk-forward optimisation (120-session training window, 20-session test window, rolled forward). Weight stability tracked across folds.
Validation
Run on simulated data with a known embedded signal.
score = w * sA * z(A) + (1 - w) * sB * z(B)
position = sign(score) if |score| > threshold else 0

The framework recovered the embedded signal (+5.1% gross). At 1 bp round-trip cost, net return was −10.5%. Optimal weight varied across folds (standard deviation 0.30).

12

EIA storage release event study

56% continuation

Release-minute move averaged 0.63%, about 37× a typical minute. Over 52 releases, the initial 5-minute direction persisted for 3 hours 56% of the time (correlation +0.15). The confidence interval (42% to 70%) includes 50%; multi-year testing is required.

14

Weekly natural gas options, Thursday to Thursday

Capital lost

ATM one-week options bought after the EIA release, priced at 55% implied volatility with a 5% spread per side, +100% take-profit.

Average option cost of approx. 3.0% of spot against an average absolute weekly move of 3.8%. The −10% stop sat inside the approx. 9.5% round-trip spread and was triggered by execution costs alone.

Findings

  • No combination of the two price factors produced a hit ratio above 50% in any market tested.
  • Net performance was driven more by turnover and transaction costs than by hit ratio.
  • Asymmetric exits lowered the break-even hit ratio from 50% to 33% in higher-volatility markets.
  • Long options underperformed the underlying in every configuration tested.
  • Leverage scaled returns and drawdowns without changing expectancy.
  • The strongest result came from a fundamental catalyst, not from price history.

Exit logic (Python) 
def simulate_exit(i0, d, E, atr):
    stop = E - d*STOP_ATR*atr
    tp   = E + d*TP_ATR*atr
    H, L = Hi[i0+1:end+1], Lo[i0+1:end+1]
    sl  = (L <= stop) if d > 0 else (H >= stop)
    tph = (H >= tp)   if d > 0 else (L <= tp)
    if first(sl) <= first(tph):
        return d*(stop-E)/E - costs
    half  = 0.5 * d*(tp-E)/E
    run   = running_best(prices after tp)
    trail = max(E, run - TRAIL_ATR*atr)
    return half + 0.5*d*(exit_at(trail)-E)/E - costs

Nietzsche, Natyashastra and the Indian Consumer

Examines why Western consumer firms have repeatedly misjudged the Indian market. The essay draws on Nietzsche's distinction between Apollonian and Dionysian modes and on the theory of rasa in Bharata's Natyashastra to argue that Indian consumption is collective and experience-led rather than individual and utility-led, with implications for pricing, positioning and market entry.

"They brought Apollo to a Dionysian market."
